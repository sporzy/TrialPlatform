# Fair KM: the story behind the brand

**The problem.** Trail running has become expensive. The big international circuits and the famous races now charge more every year. You pay for the brand, the lottery and the hype, not for the mountain. Meanwhile, hundreds of small, well-run races charge a fair price for the same effort: same climbs, same trails, same finish-line feeling. Nobody shows them side by side, so runners don't know they exist.

**What Fair KM does.** Fair KM is a curated guide to trail races that are fairly priced. It measures every race in a simple, honest way. Climbing counts, so every 100 m of elevation gain equals one extra kilometre. That gives you the "km-effort" (an existing ITRA measure). Divide the entry fee by it and you get the cost per km-effort, so a short flat race and a long mountain ultra can be compared fairly. Each race then gets one colour at a glance: green for a fair price, yellow for above the threshold, red for big-circuit prices.

Around the data there's a community. Each race has its own space where runners who have actually finished it share real advice: lift sharing, course recces, what the aid stations are really like.

**What the brand stands for.**

- **Fairness.** It isn't "cheap" or "budget". It's the right price for the effort. We're not against big races; we just measure them honestly.
- **Honesty and precision.** We use official sources only and never estimate. If something isn't verified, we say so. The tone is calm and factual, like a good measuring tool.
- **Effort.** The kilometre is earned. The climb is part of the price.
- **Runners, not marketing.** It's independent, has no conflicts of interest, and is made by trail runners for trail runners.

**The name.** "Fair" means fair or just. "KM" is the kilometre, and especially the km-effort, the unit everything is measured in. Together they mean a fair price for every kilometre you run.

**Personality.** Grounded, trustworthy, quietly confident and a little nerdy about measurement. Think of a well-made field instrument, a trail marker or a price tag that tells the truth. It shouldn't feel loud, extreme or "epic".

## Logo direction

**Possible ideas to explore** (suggestions, not requirements):

- A **trail marker or blaze**: the painted stripe on a rock or tree that tells you you're on the right path.
- A **ruler or measuring scale**, with ticks that turn into an elevation profile.
- A **price tag** whose outline is a mountain profile.
- **"KM" built from climbing**: the letters, or a mark beside them, rising like D+.
- **Balance or equality**: the idea of something weighed fairly, without using legal scales.

**Must-haves:**

- A **wordmark "Fair KM"** plus a **compact symbol** that works alone as a favicon (16 px) and an app icon. A PWA version is planned.
- It works in **one colour** and on **light and dark** backgrounds.
- It's **language-neutral**, because the site is in Italian, English, French, German and Spanish.
- **No currency symbols** (€, £, $). Prices are always shown in the race's local currency, so the brand mustn't favour one.

**Avoid:**

- The generic mountain-peak triangle on its own. Every outdoor brand uses it.
- The aggressive, neon "extreme sport" look of the big race circuits.
- Discount or bargain imagery such as "SALE", percent signs or piggy banks. We mean fair, not cheap.

**Existing visual cues** from the current landing page, which can be refined:

- Brand green: `#1d5a36` (light theme) / `#a8e2bd` (dark theme).
- Warm paper backgrounds: `#f4f0e6` / `#fbf9f3`.
- Status colours: green = fair price, yellow = above threshold, red `#c0392b` = big circuits.
- Fonts in use: **Archivo** (variable width, for headings) and **IBM Plex Mono** (for numbers and measurements).
